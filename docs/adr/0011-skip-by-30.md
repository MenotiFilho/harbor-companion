# Skip ±30s is a reducer decision, and rapid taps may coalesce

The remote wire has no relative seek — only an absolute
`{action:'seek', positionSec}` (`docs/wire-contract-research.md`). The refresh
adds ±30 s transport buttons, so the jump must be computed client-side. We
decided a pure **`SkipBy(seconds)`** reducer event reads the latest playback
position and duration from state, clamps the result to `[0, duration]`, and
emits the existing absolute `seek`. Because snapshots land roughly every 400 ms,
two taps inside one snapshot window compute from the same position and
**coalesce**; we accept that instead of keeping a pending local offset, which
would break the app's host-authoritative rule.

## Decision

- `SkipBy(seconds)` lives in the remote reducer; the controller stays a drain,
  not a decision point.
- Clamp: never below 0; never above `duration` when the duration is known.
- Coalescing of taps inside one snapshot window is accepted and documented; no
  pending-offset state.
- Buttons are disabled without held media; skip works while paused.
- UI: a five-button transport row `[prev] [-30] [play] [+30] [next]`.

## Considered Options

- **Controller computes and dispatches `Seek`** — rejected: moves the rule out
  of the pure, tested seam.
- **Pending local offset to avoid coalescing** — rejected: invents playback
  state on the phone, against the host-authoritative posture (ADR-0005).
- **Server-side relative seek** — outside the app; at most a host follow-up.

## Consequences

- Two quick `+30` taps can total `+30`; a tap after the next snapshot behaves
  normally.
- `remote_reducer_test` covers clamp and coalesce; `remote_screen_test` grows
  transport assertions.
